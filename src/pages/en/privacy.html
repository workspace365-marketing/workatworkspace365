Privacy for job applicants

This is how we handle your data when you apply to us.

Who is responsible?

New Day at Work B.V., trading as Workspace 365, Berencamperweg 6D, 3861 MC Nijkerk, the Netherlands, is responsible for your data when you apply to us.

What data do we process?

The data you share with us yourself: your name, email address, phone number, country, CV, motivation, your LinkedIn profile if you add it, and what you tell us in conversations.

What do we use it for?

To assess your application and stay in touch with you. You ask us to consider you and we want to find the right colleague. Our legal bases are steps taken at your request before an employment contract, and our legitimate interest.

Do we use AI?

Yes. We always read your CV and motivation ourselves. We also regularly use AI as an extra check. For that we use Claude, from Anthropic, with a business account. Your data stays within the EU and is not used to train Claude. A colleague always makes the decision. We do not make decisions based solely on automated processing.

Who sees your data?

The colleagues involved in your application: Talent, the team lead and colleagues who join interviews. Your data is stored in Recruitee, our recruitment system. Recruitee processes it on our behalf. The servers are in Germany.

A message sent through the contact form on our careers site arrives via Netlify, our form service, and is forwarded to us by email. Netlify is a US company. It processes data under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and with standard contractual clauses.

How long do we keep it?

Four weeks after your application is closed. After that, we delete your data. If you join us, part of your data moves to your personnel file. Other retention periods apply there.

What are your rights?

You can access, correct or delete your data. You can also ask us to restrict the processing, object to it or have your data transferred. Email us at operations@workspace365.net. We reply within one month.

Do you disagree with how we handle your data? Then you can file a complaint with the Dutch Data Protection Authority.
